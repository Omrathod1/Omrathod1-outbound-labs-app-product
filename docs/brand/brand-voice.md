# How Outbound Labs sounds (brand voice v1.0)

> Source: `docs/originals/brand-voice-v1.0.pdf` (November 2026, Rathod Group of Companies Private Limited), transcribed to Markdown on 2026-11-27.

The rules for every word we publish: website, cold emails, LinkedIn, blog, product, support and sales. Use it to write, edit and brief anyone writing for us, people or AI.

## 1. Who we sound like

The founder who just booked the meeting. We talk like a calm, competent operator explaining to another founder how outbound actually gets done. We've done the work, we know the numbers, and we don't need to shout.

Plain, specific and short. We promise outcomes we control, like meetings, approvals and safety, and we show the work instead of hyping it.

### Four voice principles

1. **Plain: common words, short sentences.** Write so a busy founder gets it in one read. If a word needs a glossary, swap it. "Find your buyers", not "ICP enrichment orchestration".
2. **Specific: numbers over adjectives.** "750 free emails, no card" beats "a generous free trial". "Auto-pause above 2% bounce" beats "built-in safety". Every claim should be checkable.
3. **Calm: confidence without noise.** The agents do the work; we don't need exclamation marks to prove it. No hype words, no countdowns, no fake urgency.
4. **Honest: say the limits out loud.** Sample data is labelled as sample. We never invent numbers, customers or reviews. We say what the product doesn't do, such as cold calls, and who should pick something else.

### Personality, on a scale

| We are more | Than          | Why |
|-------------|---------------|-----|
| Direct      | Polite filler | Our buyers are busy; the answer goes first. |
| Expert      | Academic      | We use the words sales teams use on Reddit and in Slack, not analyst jargon. |
| Warm        | Chummy        | "You" and "we", contractions, no slang or memes. |
| Confident   | Boastful      | We take positions and say what we'd skip, but we never claim to be "the best". |
| Careful     | Fearful       | Safety (approvals, warm mailboxes, auto-pause) is a feature we're proud of, not a disclaimer. |

## 2. Words

| Instead of | Write |
|------------|-------|
| Leverage AI-powered hyper-personalisation at scale. | Every first line is written from something real about the buyer. |
| Supercharge your pipeline today! | Paste your website. See your first campaigns in about ten minutes. |
| Enterprise-grade deliverability infrastructure. | Sends go from separate, warmed mailboxes. Your main domain stays safe. |
| Fully autonomous, set it and forget it. | The agents do the work. You approve every campaign before it sends. |
| Guaranteed meetings / 10x your replies. | We book the meetings; you take them. (Never promise a reply or meeting rate.) |
| Submit | Join the waitlist |

**Words we use:** buyers, meetings, campaigns, approve, warmed mailboxes, first line, reply, booked, agents (Researcher, Strategist, Prospector, Writer, Sender, Closer), outbound, pipeline.

**Words we never use:** delve, landscape, realm, navigate the, unlock, leverage (as a verb), seamless, robust, game-changer, revolutionize, elevate, empower, harness, supercharge, 10x, cutting-edge, "it's important to note", "in conclusion", "at the end of the day".

### Patterns that read as AI-written: avoid them

- "Whether you're a … or a …" openers, and rhetorical-question intros.
- "Not just X, but Y" constructions.
- Everything in threes. Use a list only when it's a real list (steps, options, checks).
- Three sentences in a row with the same shape. Mix short and long.
- A closing paragraph that only repeats what came before.
- More than one em dash per 300 words. Prefer full stops and commas.

### House style

| Item | Rule |
|------|------|
| Company name | "Outbound Labs": two words, title case. Only the logo is lower case. Never "OL" or "OutboundLabs". |
| Agent names | Capitalised as names: the Writer, the Closer. |
| Numbers | Figures for all numbers in product and marketing copy: 5,000 emails, $149 a month, 2%. |
| Prices | "$149 a month", not "$149/mo" in sentences (tables may abbreviate). |
| Headings | Sentence case. Blog H2s are questions people actually ask. |
| Punctuation | No exclamation marks or emoji in product copy. Oxford comma optional; be consistent within a piece. |
| Spelling | One spelling system per piece. Website and blog currently use US spelling for prices and product terms. |

## 3. Tone by channel

Our voice never changes. Our tone shifts with where the reader is and what they need.

| Channel | Tone | Example |
|---------|------|---------|
| Website | Confident, outcome first, short lines. | "We book your sales meetings. You just show up." |
| Product UI | Quiet and exact. Say what happened and what's next. | "4 campaigns waiting for your approval." · "Paused: bounce rate passed 2%." |
| Cold email (ours) | One person to one person. Under 90 words, one ask, no links in step one. | "Saw you're hiring two SDRs. Before you do, would it help to see what six agents book in a month?" |
| LinkedIn | Founder in public. A real lesson, a number, a position. | "We stopped sending on Mondays. Here's what our reply data said." |
| Blog | Practitioner to practitioner. Answer first, sourced numbers, take positions, admit trade-offs. | "Can AI book sales meetings? Yes, for email replies. Here's where it slips." |
| Sales calls and deck | Plain and honest about fit. Ask what they spend today, then compare. | "If your buyers only answer the phone, a calling agency is the better buy." |
| Support | Warm, fast, owns the problem. Steps, not essays. | "That's on us. Your campaign is paused and nothing else will send. Here's the fix." |
| Error states | Say what broke, whether anything sent, and the next step. | "We couldn't verify 208 addresses, so we dropped them. Nothing was sent to them." |

### Writing for AI answers and search

- Answer first. The first 40 to 60 words under any heading answer it on their own; that's the passage AI Overviews and chatbots lift.
- Name the subject. Write "Outbound Labs" or "the Sender", not "it" or "this", so each passage stands alone.
- Cite every number with its source and date. If you can't source it, leave it out.
- Be fair to competitors. State only what they publish, dated. Balanced pages get cited; one-sided pages don't.

## 4. Messaging

### Lines we use, word for word

| Use | Line |
|-----|------|
| Tagline | We book your sales meetings. You just show up. |
| One-liner | AI agents that find your buyers, write the emails and fill your calendar. |
| Description | Paste your website. AI agents find your buyers, write the emails, send them and fill your calendar. No SDRs, no lead lists, no setup. |
| Offer | 750 free emails. No card. Nothing sends until you approve. |
| Entity line (profiles, listings, press) | Outbound Labs is an autonomous AI outbound platform. Six AI agents find a company's buyers, write and send personalised cold emails from warmed mailboxes, and book meetings, with a person approving every campaign. |
| Boilerplate | Outbound Labs is an autonomous outbound platform. Six AI agents research a company's market, find decision makers, write and send personalised emails from warmed mailboxes, and book the meetings. Outbound Labs is built by Rathod Group of Companies Private Limited. |

### Messaging pillars

- **Outcome: meetings, not tools.** One website in, booked meetings out. Six agents run research, lists, writing, sending and replies.
- **Control: autonomous, never unsupervised.** You approve every campaign, in the app, by email or in Slack. Pause anything at any time.
- **Price: public, monthly, low.** From $149 a month for 5,000 emails, while most AI SDRs hide prices or charge five figures a year.

### Claims: approved, and off limits

**Say plainly**

- Six agents: Researcher, Strategist, Prospector, Writer, Sender, Closer
- You approve every campaign (app, email or Slack)
- Trial: 750 emails free, no card
- Growth $149 a month, 5,000 emails, $0.03 per extra
- Scale $499 a month, 25,000 emails, $0.02 per extra
- 5 / 25 warmed mailboxes; 1 / 5 LinkedIn accounts
- 1,000 / 10,000 website visitors identified
- Up to 40 emails per inbox per day; auto-pause above 2% bounce
- Contact data comes from B2B data provider APIs, billed by the provider
- Raw visit data deleted after 90 days; script waits for cookie consent where required
- Early access, with a waitlist

**Never say**

- Any reply rate, meeting rate or "guaranteed" result
- Customer names, logos, quotes or case studies we can't show
- That we make cold calls (we work over email and LinkedIn)
- That lead data is free or included in the plan
- Discount language ("10% off", "2 months free") in new content
- "The best", "#1", or comparisons we can't source
- Anything about a competitor that it hasn't published, undated
- Rating or user-count claims without a verifiable source

## 5. Before and after

**Homepage hero**

- Before: Unlock the power of AI-driven outbound and revolutionize your sales pipeline with our cutting-edge autonomous SDR platform!
- After: We book your sales meetings. You just show up. Paste your website, approve the campaigns, take the calls.
- Why: outcome first, plain words, no hype, and the reader knows the first step.

**LinkedIn post opener**

- Before: In today's fast-paced sales landscape, it's important to note that personalization is not just nice to have, but essential.
- After: Our Writer agent drafted 3,104 first lines last week. The ones that mentioned a job post got the replies. Here's the pattern.
- Why: a concrete number and a promise of something useful. (Use real numbers only; this one is illustrative.)

**Product notification**

- Before: Oops! Something went wrong with your campaign 😬 Please try again later!
- After: "Teams outgrowing spreadsheets" is paused. The bounce rate passed 2% on 2 mailboxes, so we stopped sending. Nothing else went out. Review the list to resume.
- Why: what happened, whether anything sent, and the next step.

## 6. Checklist: before anything ships

1. Does the first sentence answer the reader's real question?
2. Is every number sourced and dated, or one of our approved product facts?
3. Have you removed every banned word and AI pattern in section 2?
4. Would a founder say this out loud to another founder?
5. Is there one clear next step, and only one?
6. Does it promise only what we control: meetings booked, approvals, safety?
7. Is "Outbound Labs" spelled right, and are competitors described fairly?
